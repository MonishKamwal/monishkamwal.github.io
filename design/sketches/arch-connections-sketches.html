Four ways to show the wires

You chose "Pulled apart, live" (A in arch-exploded-sketches.html), and the wires between the sheets get lost. They were thin, they were drawn under the sheets, and they faded out when a sheet slid forward — which is the moment you want to see what it connects to. These are four ways to put the connections forward. Panel 0 is A as it stands.

What all four change. The wires are drawn over the sheets. A sheet slides out half as far, so its wires stay nearly straight. The wires of the sheet in focus turn ochre and stay at full strength; the other wires fade. Each wire has a label on a paper tag.
What all four add. Under each plane's text, a short sends / receives list: one row for each wire that leaves or reaches the plane. With a mouse, put the pointer on a row and its wire lights. On a touch screen, tap the row; tap it again to let go.
Mouse, trackpad, touch. The drawing moves only with the normal scroll. The harness cannot send real touch input, so the phone size shows the layout only.

A1

Wires in front

The simplest fix. The wires are heavier and drawn over the sheets, with a port where a wire leaves and an arrowhead where it arrives. When a plane is in focus, its wires turn ochre and keep their tags; every other wire drops back. The sends / receives rows say the same thing in words.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: it is the least new. The drawing gets busier in the overview, where all four wires and four tags show at once.

A2

The data moves

The wires become pipes: a soft wide body, an ink core, and small packets inside. The packets move with the scroll, always in the direction the data goes: logs down to Monitoring, the breach down to CI/CD, a new model up to Serving, the image down to the cluster. Nothing moves while you read.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: the most motion of the four, on top of a drawing that is already moving. The packets only move while you scroll, so a visitor who stops sees dotted lines, not flow.

A3

Crossings are stops

The wires get their own scroll steps, the way the Skills page gives each shared tool a stop. After each plane comes the wire that leaves it. On a wire step, only the two sheets it joins stay lit, the wire is ochre, and the text explains the crossing: what goes across, and why.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: four more steps, so twelve in all, which is a long walk. The wire text is mine, written from the contract; it needs your check, and the producer side has no wire text to keep it in step.

A4

Light shafts

Each connection is a volume, not a line: a translucent column the width of the parts it joins, with an open mouth on each sheet. The flywheel shaft is ochre; the others are a cool blue-grey. The shafts multiply with the sheets, so where a shaft passes a sheet you see both.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: the shafts are wide, so in the overview they cover parts of the sheets. It is the most stylized of the four and the furthest from the site’s thin ink line.

What I'd pick, and why

All four fix the base problem, because the wires now sit over the sheets and stay lit for the plane in focus. They differ in how much the connections lead.

My pick is A3: crossings are stops. Your note was that the connections get lost. In A1, A2 and A4 they are better drawn, but they are still the supporting cast of a plane. A3 is the only version where a connection is the subject, with its own step, its own two lit sheets and its own text. The loop is the story of this platform, and the wires are the loop. A3 already uses A1's drawing (wires in front, ochre when in focus, paper tags), so it is A1 plus the stops. The cost is length: twelve steps. If that is too long, A1 alone is the lightest fix, and the sends / receives rows carry the connections in words.

A2's moving packets could join A3 later, on the wire steps only, where motion along the wire is the point. I would not use them on every step.
