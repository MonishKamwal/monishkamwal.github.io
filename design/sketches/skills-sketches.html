Four ways to show it

Home §5 as it stands is four lines of tool names and nothing else — the only section on the page without something to look at, and the tools it lists were already on the Architecture ring one section earlier. These are four stylized replacements, each on §5's own rose ground, each boxed at one screen so nothing is judged at the wrong size. Panel 0 is today's version, kept as the control. Pick one and I'll build it into the prototype.

What every version says — the same five things, in the same order, so the comparison is about form only. The content shift underneath all four: what you can do comes first, the tools come second. That is what stops §5 repeating §3 — the ring shows the tools, this section shows the competence they add up to. Wording is placeholder; the structure is what's under review.
Mouse, trackpad, touch. Each panel has a hover payoff, so each needs an answer for a screen with no pointer. On touch every payoff rests on — the marks sit darker, the compartments sit lifted — rather than hiding behind a gesture nobody will make on a teaser. Hover rules are fenced behind (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine) so a touch screen never inherits a state it cannot leave.

The model's world

The ground carries the drawings the model reads — ten thin ink marks, large, faint, rotated, several running off the edges. They draw themselves as the section arrives, on the same stroke-dash trick the Architecture ring uses, so §5 finally has motion of its own. The competencies sit over the top in the serif with their tools quiet underneath. Hover one and the marks nearest it come up out of the paper.

Costs: the marks are decoration, not information — a key and a sailboat say "this site is about drawing", not "he can ship a model", and a reader looking for the second may resent the first. Text sits directly over strokes, so the faint resting opacity is load-bearing: any darker and the names get harder to read. Ten hand-authored paths is ten more things to keep tidy.

B

Overlapping washes

Five soft irregular shapes in the warm family, rotated and overlapping, the colour deepening wherever two cross because they multiply rather than stack. One competency named across each shape, its tools inside, and the tools two competencies share sitting out in the crossing — ONNX between training and shipping, S3 between training and data, k6 between shipping and watching. A tool named in an overlap is named nowhere else, which is the only thing that makes the crossings mean something rather than just look nice. Hover a shape and the others step back.

Costs: it is the furthest of the four from the ink-line language the rest of the page speaks — §2, §3 and §4 are all thin black line on warm paper, and this is the one screen that isn't, and the colour has to stay this pale to avoid the one thing your taste rules out. Worst of all, the overlaps only mean anything because every shape is placed by hand — the first pass had the shared names floating on open paper — so adding one tool is a geometry job, not an edit.

C

The type case

A letterpress drawer. Irregular compartments divided by hairline ink; competencies fill the wide ones in the serif, single tool names sit in the small ones at mixed sizes and weights, and that mix is the texture. Hover lifts a compartment off the page — §4's gesture, borrowed.

D

Oversized, cropped

One enormous serif line per competency, each running off both edges of the screen, offset so no two start together. The tools ride small in the gaps. You read it as texture first and words second — the most gestural of the four, and the closest to a magazine spread. Hover a line and it slides a little further into view.

Costs: the least readable at a glance — a cropped word is a word you have to finish yourself, five times in a row, and a recruiter skimming for "does he know Terraform" is the reader least willing to do that. What I'd pick, and why

Having built them: A and D are the two worth arguing about. A is the only one that earns its decoration from something already true about the site — the model in §2 reads hand-drawn strokes, so strokes belong on this page and nowhere else — and it is the only one that gives §5 motion of its own without borrowing §3's ring or §4's line. D is the one that actually looks like the magazine you're after; it is also the one that makes a reader work hardest, and it pushed the tool names down to the smallest type on the sheet. C is the best information and the worst moment — it would be a fine skills page and is too much furniture for a teaser. B is the odd one out: it is the only panel that stops speaking the page's ink-on-paper language, and it needs its geometry hand-held forever.

If you want one sentence: A if §5 should feel like part of this site, D if §5 should be the moment someone remembers.
