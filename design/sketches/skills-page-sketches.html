Sketches · the Skills page

Four ways to open the map

Home §5 now shows five competencies as overlapping washes, and its "See all skills" shape leads to this page. The Skills page in sections-prototype.html is still the July version: a list of tool names, each with one line and one link. It does not look like the place that the visitor just left. These are four new versions of the full page. Each one is a working page in a screen-shaped box. Scroll inside the box. Panel 0 is the current page, kept as the control.

Decision, 2026-10-02: A was chosen. It is built into the Skills page in sections-prototype.html. This sheet stays as the record of the options.
What every version keeps. The July rule "skills with receipts": each tool has one line that says what it did in this project, and one link to the proof. There are no bars, levels, or ratings. The proof links go to both modes: Architecture and Journey, and also Quality, Monitoring, and Performance. The tools, lines, and links are placeholders. The structure is what is under review.
What every version adds. The Home §5 content: five competencies, the tools inside each one, and the four shared tools (S3, ONNX, k6, GitHub Actions). A shared tool gets its own receipt, because it is the only tool that does work for two competencies.
Mouse, trackpad, touch. All four versions move only with the normal scroll. No version takes control of the scroll, so all three input types get the same page. Use the switch to see each version at phone size. The harness cannot send real touch input, so the phone size here shows the layout, not the feel of a finger.

The current page

The control, taken from sections-prototype.html. Nine tools on alternate sides, then a divider, then the long tail of tool names.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: this is the "list with a line next to it" that the Home section was rebuilt to get away from. It does not use the competencies or the shared tools, so a visitor who arrives from the washes finds a different page.

A

Into the map

The page opens on the Home §5 map, with the same shapes at the same places. The visitor then goes into it. The map holds still while the text scrolls, and each scroll step moves the view to the next stop: a competency, then the crossing to the next one, then that competency. The crossings are stops too, so S3, ONNX, k6 and GitHub Actions each get their own receipt. A click or a tap on a shape goes to its stop. At the end, the view moves back out and shows the whole map again.

Scroll inside the box, or click a shape.

Costs: this is the most motion on any inner page, and all of it depends on the scroll. Eleven stops is a long walk for a visitor who wants only to find "Terraform". On a phone the map takes the top half of the screen, so the reading area is small. The map must also stay correct on two pages now: a change to Home §5 is a change here.

B

The map, unrolled

The same five shapes, laid out down the page as chapters. Each shape goes off the left or right edge. Two shapes that share a tool overlap at the edge between their chapters, and the shared tool sits in that overlap with its receipt. Shipping shares tools with two competencies below it, so the page forks there: Watching and Front sit side by side under it, and they do not touch each other. The page layout is the same graph as the map.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: on a phone the fork cannot stay side by side. Front goes under Watching and loses its contact with Shipping, so GitHub Actions stands in Shipping only. That is a small false statement on the smallest screen. The washes are behind the text on the full page, so the warm colour covers more area than it does on Home.

C

The receipts, on the table

The word "receipts" taken at its word. Each tool is a small paper slip, in the card style of the Journey and Architecture pages, put down at a small angle on its competency's shape. A shared tool is a slip with an ink top edge, put across the overlap of its two shapes. The shapes use the portrait arrangement from Home §5, made large, so the table is about three screens deep. With a mouse, a slip straightens and lifts when the pointer is on it.

Scroll inside the box. Put the pointer on a slip.

Costs: fifteen slips on five shapes is busy, and each slip is placed by hand, the same permanent cost as the Home washes. On a phone the table cannot stay a table: it becomes a stack of slips, one competency after the next, with the same fork problem as B. The angle of the slips is the most playful thing on the site. It is near the line, so judge it against "never childish".

D

Oversized, drifting

Each competency name is set very large in Bodoni italic, wider than the screen, on a band of its wash. When the page scrolls, each name moves slowly to the side, in the opposite direction from the name before it. This is the "drift" from the July spec, on the names instead of the lines. Under each name, a plain ledger: tool, what it did, and the proof. A shared tool is in the ledger of both of its competencies, with an ochre mark.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: it has the weakest link to the Home map. The shapes become straight bands, and the crossings become a mark in a table. The ledger is the easiest of the four to read, and the most like a list. The large names do the work that the shapes do in A, B and C.

What I'd pick, and why

Now that they are built, A and B are the two to choose between. Both keep the Home map as the structure of the page, and both make the crossings into real places with their own receipt. C and D are each strong in one way and weak in another. C's slips are the most charming thing on the sheet, and also the busiest, and the angle is near the "childish" line. D has the best type on the sheet, but it drops the map, and the map is what Home §5 gave the visitor.

My pick is B. A visitor on a skills page looks for a name such as "Terraform" and for its proof. B lets them read with a normal scroll, and the graph is still the layout of the page. A is the better first moment: the visitor lands on the map they just left and goes into it. But A has eleven stops of camera motion, it adds to the arrival line and the edge blur, and its phone reading area is small. If you like A's first screen, B can open on the full Home map and then unroll it. That is a small change, and I can build it.

One sentence: B if the page is for reading, A if the page is for remembering.
