Sketches · the Architecture page

Four ways to tell the machine

The Architecture page in sections-prototype.html is a flowchart of white tiles, written around the July placeholder chapters. The platform now publishes the real text in architecture.json, and that text tells a sharper story. These are four new versions of the full page. Each one is a working page in a screen-shaped box. Scroll inside the box. Open the current page next to this sheet to compare.

The content is real. Every version uses the five sections of the live contract: System overview, The retrain flywheel, The quality gate, Ephemeral Kubernetes, and Evidence & drift. The text is the contract's text with light cuts. One change: the contract has thumbs-up and thumbs-down emoji, and the site has no emoji, so the text says "a yes" and "a no". That wording must also change on the producer side.
What every version brings back. Two facts that the Home ring had to drop. The cluster exists for about 30 minutes a month, and a crippled 0.50 model is blocked before it can ship. These are the facts that people remember.
Mouse, trackpad, touch. All four versions move only with the normal scroll. No version takes control of the scroll. Use the switch to see each version at phone size. The harness cannot send real touch input, so the phone size shows the layout only.
A

The turning ring

The page opens on the ring from Home §3, with the same four planes and the same logos. Then the ring turns like a dial. Each scroll step brings the next plane round to face the text. The flywheel section turns the ring one full time, because that is what the section describes. At the Build stop, the ring grows two marks: a dashed dead end for the blocked 0.50 model, and an ochre arc for the 30 minutes a month.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: it is the same pattern as the new Skills page (a fixed picture, text scrolling past). That is good for the family and bad for surprise. The ring holds four planes and not the detail inside them, so the text does most of the work. Rotation is the motion with the most risk of motion sickness. Reduced motion must jump, and it does.

B

One month, to scale

The architecture as a timetable. One month runs left to right in four lanes, one for each plane, and the scroll moves time forward. Serving is one unbroken line, because it is always on. CI/CD is a burst for each merge, and one merge ends at a bar: the 0.50 model, blocked. Monitoring is a report every week, and one report opens an issue that starts a retrain. The Kubernetes lane is empty, except for a sliver too thin to see. When the sliver reaches the ochre line, time slows and a lens opens on those 30 minutes: apply, exercise, destroy.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: it is the least like a diagram. A visitor who wants "what talks to what" gets "what happens when" instead, and must build the map in their head. The month is illustrative (merge days, drift days and the date of the cluster are invented), and it must say so. The page scrolls sideways under a vertical scroll, which some people find strange the first time.

C

The exploded drawing

The four planes, drawn the way an architecture book draws a building: as stacked sheets, pulled apart. The top of the page shows the stack closed. Below it, the sheets come apart one by one as you scroll, each with its parts on it and its text beside it. Ink wires cross between the sheets where data crosses between planes. The ochre wire is the flywheel: it climbs from CI/CD back up to Serving. The Kubernetes sheet is drawn dashed, because most of the time it does not exist.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: it is the most work to keep correct. Every part has a hand-placed position on its sheet, and every wire depends on those positions. On a phone, the sheets stack one under the next and the long wires become short labels, so the phone loses the picture of the loop. The angled drawing is the furthest from the site's flat ink line.

D

Follow the doodle

The page follows one drawing through the whole loop, on one ink line, in the line grammar of the Journey page. The drawing rides the line as you scroll, and its paper tag changes at each stop: guessed, graded, in the pile, in the training split. At the gate the line forks. A ghost 0.50 model takes the left branch and stops at a bar. Your drawing's model takes the right one and ships. A dashed loop leaves Deploy for the monthly cluster. At the bottom, the line turns back up: round again.

Scroll inside the box.

Costs: it is a story, not a map, so it never shows the whole system at once. The overview from Stage 4.7 is gone unless it comes back as a small figure. A doodle on the page is the closest of the four to "playful", so it must stay in ink and stay small.

What I'd pick, and why

Having built them, each version is good at one thing. A keeps the hand-off from Home: the visitor clicks the ring and lands on the ring. C is the clearest map, and the only version where you can see what talks to what. D is the most personal, and it uses the site's own line. B is the one that shows what is special about this platform.

My pick is B. The claims that make this platform unusual are about time and money: serving costs about $0, the cluster lives for 30 minutes a month, and drift is checked every Monday. Every other version states those facts. B is the only one that draws them, so a visitor sees how small that sliver is before they read the number. The lens on the 30 minutes is the moment people will remember. Its weakness is that it is not a map. To fix that, B can open on the Home ring for one screen, so the hand-off from Home stays, and then unroll into the month. I can build that.

If you want one sentence: B to be remembered, C to be understood, D to feel it, A to stay close to Home.
